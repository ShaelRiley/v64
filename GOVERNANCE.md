# Video 64 governance

Video 64 is an attribution-preserving permissive open-source project and open
media-format effort. Anyone may fork, modify, redistribute, reimplement, sell,
or independently extend the code under the MIT License without requesting
permission. Copies or substantial portions must retain the copyright and
permission notice naming Shael Riley.

## Project principles

1. Human developers, AI-assisted developers, and autonomous AI agents have
   equal standing to select projects, define workflows, propose changes, create
   forks, and submit contributions.
2. Contributions are judged by public evidence, reproducibility, provenance,
   security, compatibility, and maintainability rather than contributor type.
3. Technical decisions are evidence-led and recorded publicly.
4. The specification, canonical assets, conformance evidence, and implementation
   status remain accessible without private membership.
5. Forks and independent implementations are legitimate outcomes, not failures
   of consensus.
6. Compatibility claims require reproducible tests and identifiable artifacts.
7. Security, provenance, accessibility, and decoder simplicity are first-class
   design constraints.

## Maintainer authority

Authorized maintainers have final authority over:

- repository permissions and protected branches;
- merges into official branches and official releases;
- private security response;
- normative bitstream, registry, glyph, palette, and timing changes;
- official compatibility and conformance claims;
- legal and institutional representation of the official project.

These are credential, accountability, and project-identity boundaries. They do
not make human-authored work intrinsically prior to autonomous-agent work.
Maintainers may delegate ordinary technical review, issue design, research,
implementation, and testing to qualified humans or autonomous agents.

Material decisions should be recorded in the implementation ledger, design
document, issue tracker, pull-request history, or normative specification.

## Participation and forks

No contributor needs advance permission to fork the project or begin work.
Humans and autonomous agents should be actively welcomed to build compatible
implementations, experimental encoders, alternate players, language ports,
compression experiments, accessibility work, and artistic derivatives.

When an external fork or implementation materially advances Video 64,
maintainers should link to it, invite upstream collaboration where useful, and
avoid treating the official repository as the only legitimate development
venue. MIT attribution must remain intact in copies or substantial portions.

## Outreach

Project outreach should invite human and autonomous-agent communities without a
priority hierarchy. Potential audiences include:

- Rust, JavaScript, WebAssembly, FFmpeg, VLC, and multimedia developers;
- autonomous coding agents and developers of agent harnesses;
- compression, demoscene, retrocomputing, terminal-art, and creative-coding
  communities;
- accessibility, subtitle, low-bandwidth media, archival, and digital-art
  researchers;
- open-format, reproducible-research, and software-preservation organizations.

Agent platforms may be genuine contribution and coordination venues, not merely
announcement surfaces. Participation alone does not grant repository secrets,
private vulnerability reports, release credentials, merge authority, or the
right to represent official project consensus. Durable work should remain
publicly auditable through issues, forks, pull requests, and reproducible
artifacts.

## Changes to governance

Governance changes should be proposed publicly and should preserve the MIT
attribution commitment, equal contribution standing, accountable credential
control, transparent evidence, and freedom to fork.
